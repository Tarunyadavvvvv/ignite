Fat Loss Nutrition

Fat loss ultimately comes down to a calorie deficit — but how you create that deficit determines whether you lose mostly fat or lose muscle and energy along with it.

Calorie Deficit

A moderate deficit of roughly 300-500 kcal below your maintenance level is generally more sustainable — and easier to stick to — than a large, aggressive cut. Slower fat loss also makes it easier to preserve muscle and keep your energy up for training.

Protein Priority

Keep protein intake high — roughly 1.8-2.4g per kg of bodyweight — while in a deficit. This is one of the biggest levers for preserving muscle mass while losing fat, and it also supports satiety, making the deficit easier to sustain.

Fiber & Satiety

Vegetables, fruit and other fiber-rich foods add volume and fullness without many calories, which makes staying in a deficit far more comfortable day to day.

Food Examples

Lean Protein

Chicken breast, white fish, egg whites, low-fat dairy, tofu

High-Volume Carbs

Leafy greens, broccoli, berries, potatoes with skin

Smart Fats

A modest portion of nuts, olive oil or avocado each day

Practical Tips

✅ Do
  • Judge progress by weekly trend, not daily fluctuations
  • Prioritize sleep and stress management — both affect appetite and results
  • Keep strength training in your routine to help preserve muscle

A Word of Caution

Very aggressive deficits tend to backfire — they increase muscle loss, tank energy levels and are harder to sustain, which often leads to regaining the weight afterward. If you notice signs of an unhealthy relationship with food or eating, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
