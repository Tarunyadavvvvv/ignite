Maintenance Nutrition

Maintenance means eating roughly the number of calories your body uses each day — holding steady while you focus purely on training, building habits, or simply taking a break from actively gaining or losing.

Finding Your Maintenance Level

The most reliable way to find your maintenance calories isn't a formula — it's data. Track your food intake and bodyweight consistently for 2-3 weeks. If your weight stays roughly stable, your average intake over that period is a good estimate of your maintenance level.

The Balanced Plate

A simple mental model for most meals: roughly a palm-sized portion of protein, a fist-sized portion of vegetables, a cupped-hand portion of carbohydrates, and a thumb-sized portion of fats. It's not exact, but it removes the need to weigh and measure everything.

Staying Flexible

Maintenance is a good phase to practice a flexible approach — sometimes summarized as the "80/20 rule": if roughly 80% of your intake comes from nutritious, whole foods, the other 20% has plenty of room for the foods you simply enjoy.

Food Examples

Protein

Any lean or moderate-fat protein source you enjoy and can eat consistently

Carbohydrates

A mix of whole grains, fruit and starchy vegetables

Fats

A moderate, consistent amount across most meals

Practical Tips

✅ Do
  • Use maintenance as a chance to build sustainable habits
  • Re-check your maintenance estimate every few months, or after a significant weight change
  • Treat this as a stable base you can shift from later, not a permanent plan
