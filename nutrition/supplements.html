Supplements

Supplements are exactly that — supplementary. No product on this page replaces consistent training, adequate protein, or overall calorie balance. Here's a practical, tiered way to think about them.

Worth Considering

Protein Powder

A convenient way to hit daily protein targets, not a requirement if whole food already covers it.

Creatine Monohydrate

One of the most well-studied supplements for strength and training performance.

Caffeine

Can modestly improve focus and training performance in a moderate dose.

Situational

Multivitamin

Useful if your diet has clear gaps, less necessary with a varied, whole-food diet.

Electrolytes

Helpful for long or very sweaty sessions, less necessary for short, moderate workouts.

Omega-3 / Fish Oil

Worth considering if you rarely eat fatty fish.

Not Necessary for Most People

Most proprietary "fat burner" blends, most branded pre-workout stacks beyond basic caffeine, and heavily marketed niche products rarely outperform the fundamentals — sleep, training consistency, protein and total calories. Treat aggressive marketing claims with skepticism.

A Word of Caution

Supplements are not regulated as tightly as medication in most regions. Choose products that are third-party tested where possible, and talk to a doctor or qualified healthcare professional before starting anything new — especially if you take medication or have an existing health condition.
