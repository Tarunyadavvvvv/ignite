Muscle Gain Nutrition

Building muscle takes three things working together: a controlled calorie surplus, enough protein, and progressive training. Nutrition can't force muscle to grow — but it fails to support growth remarkably easily if any of these is missing.

Calorie Surplus

Muscle gain requires slightly more energy coming in than going out. A moderate surplus of roughly 250-500 kcal above your maintenance level is enough to support new muscle growth without adding excess fat too quickly. Bigger surpluses don't build muscle faster — they mostly add fat.

Protein Target

Aim for roughly 1.6-2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day, spread across 3-5 meals. Protein provides the raw material (amino acids) your body uses to repair and build muscle tissue after training.

Carbs & Fats

Carbohydrates fuel your training sessions, especially heavier or higher-volume lifting, so don't cut them aggressively while trying to build muscle. Fats support hormone production, including hormones involved in muscle growth — don't push them too low either.

Food Examples

Protein

Chicken, eggs, fish, Greek yogurt, tofu, lentils, whey protein

Carbohydrates

Rice, oats, potatoes, whole grain bread, fruit

Fats

Nuts, olive oil, avocado, egg yolks, fatty fish

Practical Tips

✅ Do
  • Track your bodyweight trend weekly, not day to day
  • Pair the surplus with progressive overload in training
  • Give it time — meaningful muscle gain is measured in months

A Word of Caution

A large, aggressive surplus doesn't speed up muscle building — it mostly adds body fat. If your weight is climbing faster than roughly 0.5% of bodyweight per week, dial the surplus back slightly.
