Pre & Post-Workout Nutrition

Meal timing matters far less than your total daily intake — but a little planning around your training window can still improve performance and recovery.

Before Training

Eat a meal combining protein and carbohydrates roughly 1-3 hours before training. Carbs top up the energy your muscles will use, while protein primes amino acid availability. If you're training very soon, keep the meal smaller and lower in fat and fiber to avoid feeling sluggish or uncomfortable mid-session.

Hydration

Start training already well-hydrated rather than trying to catch up once you're thirsty. For most sessions under an hour, water is enough — for longer or hotter sessions, consider adding electrolytes.

After Training

Aim to eat a protein-and-carbohydrate meal within a couple of hours of finishing. The so-called "anabolic window" is wider and more flexible than it's often made out to be — what matters most is your total daily protein and calorie intake, not eating within the first 30 minutes.

Simple Examples

Before Training

Oats with fruit, or toast with eggs, roughly 1-2 hours out

After Training

A protein shake and a piece of fruit, followed by a full meal within a couple of hours

A Word of Caution

Don't stress over hitting an exact minute-by-minute timing window — your total daily nutrition matters far more than precise pre/post-workout timing. Use these guidelines as a helpful structure, not a strict rule.
